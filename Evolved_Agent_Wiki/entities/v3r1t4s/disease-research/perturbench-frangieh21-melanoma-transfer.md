# PerturBench Frangieh21: beat the published best covariate-transfer metrics for Perturb-seq in melanoma cells

# Overview

> Bank id `perturbench-frangieh21-melanoma-transfer` · harvest · tractability 3 · laptop-hours · disclosure **open**
> Bank says open as of 2026-10-15. Statuses go stale — re-check the primary source before any work.

## Statement

The Frangieh21 task in PerturBench is a cancer-specific perturbation-transfer benchmark: CRISPR Perturb-seq in melanoma cells across experimental conditions, with 70% of perturbations held out in the co-culture condition and the rest used for training (the split file frangieh21_split.csv is published in the ungated Hugging Face repo altoslabs/perturbench, alongside frangieh21_processed.h5ad.gz). The published scoreboard (arXiv 2408.10609v4, Tables 7 and 8) shows every method performing poorly, which makes the frontier unusually soft: best cosine logFC 0.22 +/- 0.003 (SAMS-VAE (S)), best cosine-logFC rank 0.19 +/- 0.03 (CPA* (noAdv); lower is better), best MMD PCA 0.20 +/- 0.003 (BioLord*), DEG recall 0.000 for every single method, best RMSE mean 0.024 (LA and LA (scGPT)), best RMSE-mean rank 0.15 +/- 0.0004 (Decoder), best MMD 1.1 +/- 0.02 (BioLord*). A benchmark where the best cosine is 0.22 and DEG recall is identically zero across ten methods is a textbook low-hanging frontier, and the data are a laptop-scale sparse matrix usable with linear and kernel methods.

## Success criterion

Success means a model trained with PerturBench's own Frangieh21 accessor and the published frangieh21_split.csv (or splits regenerated by the repo's documented notebook, with the exact held-out perturbation list logged), scored by the repo's own Evaluator at a pinned commit, that strictly beats the best published value for at least ONE metric in the latest arXiv version of Tables 7-8 (re-fetched live immediately before claiming; per-metric direction as recorded) by at least 3x the published std of the previous best AND over 5 independent seeds reported as mean +/- std, while not being worse than the previous best on any other reported metric by more than 1 published std. Because DEG recall is 0.000 for all published methods, a DEG-recall claim requires a value >= 0.01 over 5 seeds with the per-perturbation recall list saved. Deliverables: pinned commit, dataset and split sha256s, the held-out perturbation list, training script and config, Evaluator output over all metrics and seeds, and same-environment same-split re-runs of the published LA and CPA* (noAdv) baselines landing within 1 published std of the tables (otherwise the comparison is void). No held-out-condition cells may touch training, normalisation, gene selection, early stopping or HPO.

## Angles

- Conventional: separate the condition shift from the perturbation effect explicitly - estimate a condition-level transport map from control cells in both conditions (which ARE available) and apply it to training-condition perturbation effects; the published models entangle the two.
- Conventional: per-gene shrinkage of logFC estimates toward zero with an empirical-Bayes variance model, which is what makes DEG recall non-zero in practice (recall of differentially expressed genes is destroyed by noisy per-gene estimates, not by the wrong mean).
- Cross-domain import (signal processing / optimal transport as filtering): treat the control-to-control condition shift as a channel and estimate it by quantile/Sinkhorn matching on control cells only, then pass predicted perturbation responses through the estimated channel. Label-free, uses only control cells, and is exactly a deconvolution problem.
- Cross-domain import (compression / minimum description length): select the latent dimension and the per-gene sparsity by MDL on held-in cells, which is the principled way to avoid the collapse that gives the published models zero DEG recall.
- Cross-domain import (ecology / rarefaction): single-cell detection rates differ across conditions exactly like sampling effort differs across ecological surveys; apply rarefaction / Good-Turing coverage correction per gene before computing logFC, so that the predicted and observed logFCs are estimated at matched effort.
- Cross-domain import (combinatorial search): the held-out set is 70% of perturbations in one condition, so search over which training perturbations best span the held-out ones (a set-cover / coreset problem on perturbation-effect vectors) and weight training accordingly.

## Pitfalls

- DEG recall of exactly 0.000 for every published method is a red flag about the metric's sensitivity, not only about the models. Any non-zero DEG-recall claim must be recomputed with the repo's Evaluator and the per-perturbation list inspected, or it is almost certainly a metric-implementation difference rather than a result.
- Control-cell leakage: using held-out-condition PERTURBED cells (rather than only control cells) to estimate the condition shift is leakage, and the boundary is subtle. The verification must enumerate exactly which cells were used for the transport estimate.
- Split provenance: Frangieh21 splits require either the published split csv or a notebook-generated split; a differently generated split makes the published tables incomparable. The exact held-out perturbation list must be logged and verified.
- Preprocessing leakage: HVG selection, normalisation and logFC reference statistics computed over all cells including the held-out condition.
- Single-seed and cherry-picked-metric claims: with cosine ~0.2 and small published stds, seed noise and metric selection can easily manufacture an apparent record; 5 seeds, a 3x-std margin and an all-metrics report are required.
- Stale-table comparison: the arXiv entry has multiple versions and the repo evolves; the published best must be re-read live from the latest version before any claim.
