# PerturBench Srivatsan20: beat the published best cross-cell-line chemical-perturbation transfer in three cancer cell lines

# Overview

> Bank id `perturbench-srivatsan20-cell-line-transfer` · harvest · tractability 3 · laptop-hours · disclosure **open**
> Bank says open as of 2026-10-15. Statuses go stale — re-check the primary source before any work.

## Statement

The Srivatsan20 task in PerturBench (Altos Labs; NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks; arXiv 2408.10609v4, last updated 2025-10-24) asks a question a medicinal chemist would recognise: given how 188 compounds change gene expression in some cancer cell lines, predict what they do in a cell line where those compounds were never tested. The data are sci-Plex single-cell profiles of A549 lung adenocarcinoma, K562 chronic myelogenous leukaemia and MCF-7 breast cancer cells, with 30% of perturbations held out per cell line for validation and test. The published scoreboard is flat and low: in Table 2 the best cosine on log-fold-changes is 0.53 +/- 0.01 (SAMS-VAE* (S)), the best cosine-logFC rank is 0.09 +/- 0.004 (CPA* (noAdv); lower is better, 0.5 is random), the best MMD PCA is 0.49 +/- 0.01 (CPA* (noAdv)), and the best DEG recall is 0.007 +/- 0.002 (CPA*) - essentially zero differentially-expressed-gene recovery across every method. Table 5 adds RMSE mean best 0.017 (LA (scGPT)), RMSE-mean rank best 0.10 (CPA* (noAdv)) and MMD best 2.2 (Linear). A benchmark where ten methods recover under 1% of differentially expressed genes is an open frontier rather than a saturated leaderboard, and the data are ungated: srivatsan20_highest_dose_processed.h5ad.gz (410 MB) in the public Hugging Face repo altoslabs/perturbench, with splits generated by the package itself and the Evaluator shipped in the repo. 410 MB of sparse counts plus linear and kernel models is a CPU-only, laptop-hours problem.

## Success criterion

Success means a model trained with PerturBench's own Srivatsan20 accessor and package-generated covariate-transfer splits (train = all cell lines minus the 30% held-out perturbations per cell line; the exact held-out perturbation list per cell line logged), scored by the repository's own Evaluator at a pinned commit, that strictly beats the best published value for at least ONE metric in Table 2 or Table 5 of the latest arXiv version of PerturBench (re-fetched live immediately before claiming; cosine logFC higher is better, all other listed metrics lower is better except DEG recall which is higher) by at least 3x the published standard deviation of the previous best AND over 5 independent seeds reported as mean +/- std, while not being worse than the previous best on any other reported metric by more than 1 published std. For DEG recall, where every published method sits at or below 0.007, a claim requires >= 0.02 over 5 seeds with the per-perturbation recall list saved. Deliverables: pinned commit, dataset sha256, the per-cell-line held-out perturbation lists, training script and config, full Evaluator output over all metrics and seeds, and same-environment same-split re-runs of the published LA and Linear baselines landing within 1 published std of the tables (otherwise the comparison is void). No cell carrying a held-out perturbation may touch training, normalisation, highly-variable-gene selection, early stopping or hyperparameter search.

## Verification

Clean virtual environment, perturbench at the attacker's pinned commit, download srivatsan20_highest_dose_processed.h5ad.gz from the ungated Hugging Face dataset and compare sha256 with the manifest, regenerate the splits with the package (or load the attacker's split files and verify they match the generator and that held-out perturbations are disjoint from training per cell line), load the attacker's predicted anndata and recompute every metric with the repository's Evaluator (under an hour), re-train 2 of the 5 seeds (laptop-hours) and re-run the LA and Linear baselines to confirm the published values are reproduced. Leakage audit: normalisation and gene selection statistics computed on training cells only; control cells used exactly as the package does; no held-out-perturbation cell read outside final scoring.

## Angles

- Conventional: factorise explicitly into a cell-line context term and a compound-effect term with a low-rank interaction, which is the natural model for a 3-cell-line x 188-compound design and far cheaper than the published autoencoders.
- Conventional: per-gene empirical-Bayes shrinkage of predicted log-fold-changes, which is what makes DEG recall non-zero in practice - the published models fail on DEG recall because per-gene estimates are noisy, not because the mean direction is wrong.
- Cross-domain import (chemistry / read-across): borrow regulatory read-across methodology - predict an untested compound's effect in a new cell line from structurally and mechanistically analogous compounds with an explicit applicability domain, and abstain outside it, reporting abstention uniformly.
- Cross-domain import (coding theory / soft-decision decoding): treat each compound's measured profile in the training cell lines as a noisy codeword observation and decode the held-out cell line's response with a minimum-distance decoder over a learned metric, which gives calibrated confidence instead of a point estimate.
- Cross-domain import (statistical physics / linear response): model the cell line as a system with its own susceptibility matrix and the compound as an external field; linear-response theory then predicts transfer as a matrix product, and the susceptibility can be estimated from unperturbed and training-perturbation data only.
- Cross-domain import (ecology / rarefaction and Good-Turing): per-cell detection rates differ across cell lines like sampling effort differs across surveys; coverage-corrected log-fold-change estimation puts predicted and observed effects on matched effort before scoring.
- Anti-collapse training: optimise a listwise rank loss on perturbation identity rather than reconstruction RMSE, since the rank metric is the discriminative part of the benchmark and the paper itself documents mode collapse (Table 12) on this dataset.

## Pitfalls

- Mode collapse flatters cosine and RMSE: predicting the average perturbation response gives a respectable cosine logFC while rank metrics and DEG recall show nothing was learned. The paper quantifies collapse for this dataset in Table 12; any claim must report all metrics plus a collapse audit.
- Split regeneration mismatch: Srivatsan20 splits are produced by the package, so a different version or seed yields a different held-out set and incomparable numbers. The exact per-cell-line held-out perturbation lists must be logged and verified.
- Preprocessing leakage: highly-variable-gene selection, size-factor normalisation or control-mean statistics computed over all cells, including held-out perturbations, leaks the test distribution into training.
- Metric reimplementation: cosine logFC, the rank metric, MMD PCA and DEG recall have specific definitions in the repository's Evaluator (gene subsets, PCA dimensionality, kernel bandwidth). Recomputing them with the attacker's own code is the easiest way to manufacture a fake record.
- Single-seed wins and stale tables: published standard deviations are 1e-3 to 1e-2 while a new model's seed variance can be far larger, and PerturBench has had four arXiv versions. Five seeds, a 3x-std margin and a live re-read of the latest version are required.
- Over-claiming: better cross-cell-line transfer for 188 compounds in three immortalised cancer lines is a benchmark result about sci-Plex data, not evidence that a drug will work in a tumour.
