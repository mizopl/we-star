Abstract
Prevailing paradigms for automated mathematical and algorithmic discovery—such as AlphaTensor, FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, and PatternBoost—rely on distributed cloud clusters of GPUs or TPUs to train specialized neural policies or evolve heuristics across hundreds of thousands of evaluations. In this paper, we report the discovery and independent machine verification of over 80 new world records across eight distinct scientific disciplines, computed entirely locally over a single weekend on a standard office computer (Apple M1 Pro, 10 CPU cores, 16 GB RAM, $0 cloud compute). Our method, CLSS (Compositional Lifting and Seam Surgery), exploits a universal structural vulnerability in human-constructed mathematical record tables: to verify correctness on paper, human researchers compose smaller exact building blocks using rigid, uniform algebraic rules (Kronecker tensor products, symmetric block splits, cyclic group voltage lifts, concentric atomic shells, or unpruned digit bases), inevitably leaving localized structural redundancy—a compositional seam—along component boundaries. Concentrating local exact optimization exclusively on these boundary seams yielded certified world records across: (1) Operations Research (31 of 60 IHTC-2024 hospital timetabling instances, led by i27: 50,976 → 50,063), (2) Combinatorial Designs (20 covering design records, led by C(94,13,5) ≤ 125,184, −4,019 blocks), (3) Bioinformatics (17 DNA minimizer density records, led by (w=12,k=4) at 0.1336292920, −1.776%), (4) Nanomaterials Physics (8 putative global minima for 55-atom CunAg55−n Gupta nanoalloys in the Cambridge Cluster Database, led by Cu39Ag16 with ΔE = −1.18984440 eV), (5) Symbolic Algorithms (4 ternary fast matrix multiplication records in ℤT={−1,0,1}, led by ⟨14,15,15⟩ at rank 1,894 and ⟨9,12,14⟩ at rank 954), (6) Extremal Graph Theory (a degree–diameter (14,3) graph on N = 1,118 vertices), (7) Statistical Mechanics (exact 44-digit A054759(15) and 80-entry antidiagonal d=79 of A089980 up to 279 digits), and (8) Additive Number Theory & Erdős Problems (explicit finite-depth N=1013 lower bound C3a(V) > 1.19230254 for the Gyarmati–Hennecart–Ruzsa constant, beating the finite-certified record 1.19102809 by +0.00127445).

1. Introduction: The Seam Principle & Local Office-Computer Discovery

Across discrete mathematics, additive number theory, operations research, bioinformatics, and computational physics, public benchmark repositories track the best known explicit objects and bounds. Beating entries in these long-standing repositories has traditionally been assumed to require supercomputing clusters or specialized hardware accelerators.

The Seam Principle: When human mathematicians or algorithm engineers construct large valid objects, they must verify correctness analytically or via uniform recurrences. Consequently, they glue smaller exact objects together using rigid, uniform rules (Kronecker products, symmetric v = v1 + v2 splits, uniform cyclic group lifts ℤm, concentric core–shell atomic layers, or unpruned base-Q digit expansions). These uniform rules inevitably leave localized structural redundancy—a compositional seam—along the boundary between composed components.

Because our entire cross-disciplinary discovery campaign was executed locally over a single weekend on a standard office computer (10-core CPU, 16 GB RAM, zero cloud GPUs/TPUs), the acronym CLSS equally stands for COMPUTER LAZY SUNDAY SOLVER. Running 100% locally on an ordinary office computer carries two fundamental scientific implications: first, it demonstrates that at medium-to-large combinatorial scales (where unconstrained search spaces exceed 101000 states), isolating the low-dimensional boundary seam beats brute-force cloud compute by orders of magnitude; second, every single certificate reported here can be independently verified in under 5 seconds on any standard personal computer.

3. Domain-Specific Technical Results & Certificates

3.1. Healthcare Operations Research: 31 New World Records on IHTC-2024

The Integrated Healthcare Timetabling Competition 2024 (IHTC-2024) couples patient admission, operating theater scheduling, and room/nurse assignment across 84 shifts. Single-shift swap heuristics cannot cross the ContinuityOfCare barrier when a nurse covers k ≥ 2 shifts of a patient's stay. We introduced Compound Patient-Stay Nurse Consolidation (replacing a dropped nurse ndrop → nkeep across all shifts of a patient's stay simultaneously in O(1) incremental time, ~10 ns per check) followed by exact HiGHS MILP polishing over 6-nurse subsets across all 84 shifts. On public benchmark i27 (493 patients, 55 nurses, 26 rooms, PR #416), total cost dropped from 50,976 to 50,063 (−913 points, −11.01% on nurse-room soft penalties, 0 hard violations). Across all 60 competition instances (i01–i30, m01–m30), our solver achieved 31 new world records (including m23: −434, m16: −287, i21: −271, m27: −243, and hidden instance m28: 45,913 → 45,873, PR #431).

3.3. Additive Number Theory & Erdős Problems: Gyarmati–Hennecart–Ruzsa Constant C3a > 1.19230254

In the Erdős–Ruzsa tradition of comparing sumsets A+B and difference sets A−B, the Gyarmati–Hennecart–Ruzsa constant C3a (tracked on Terence Tao's teorth/optimizationproblems/blob/main/constants/3a.md) is certified from below by any finite set V ⊂ ℤ≥0 with 0 ∈ V and |V−V| < 2 max(V)+1 via C3a ≥ 1 + log(|V−V|/|V+V|) / log(2 max(V)+1). Prior work by Kleinwaks (2026) established an asymptotic limit of 1.19519192* as N → ∞ in even base q = 27,022, but its explicit finite-depth certificate at N = 1013 had to fall back to the large uncontrolled-carry base Q = 2B+1 = 34,065 (B = 17,032), capping the published finite-certified record at 1.19102809.

Inspecting the formal Lean 4 injectivity seam (residue_word_injective in CoreConstruction.lean), we observed that exact multinomial difference counting at finite depth N requires only that a pruned difference subset D̃ ⊆ M−M have pairwise distinct residues modulo an odd base q < 2B+1. Partitioning non-zero residues modulo odd q = 27,995 into 13,997 complementary pairs {r, q−r} and selecting the minimum digit-sum representative κ(d) on the semigroup mask M = spanℕ0({3,4}×{506,508,529}) ∩ [0, 17086] (|M| = 3,153, |S| = 18,835) realizes all 13,997 pairs, forming a complete residue system modulo 27,995 (|D̃| = 27,995 = q). Doubling V = 2U at finite depth N = 1013 (n0 = 17,074,745,018, L = 50,031,092,045,455,301, PR #485) certifies:

  • Primary Finite Certificate (B = 17,086, q = 27,995, N = 1013): C3a(V) > 1.19230254070568368329 (60-dps mpmath.iv) and > 1.19230254068351294895 (100% pure integer-interval arithmetic with Lean StirlingBounds.lean), beating 1.19102809 by +0.00127445 (12,744× the 10−7 margin; V3R1T4S PR #485, submitted to teorth/optimizationproblems as PR #236).
  • Secondary Finite Certificate on Kleinwaks's Exact Mask (B = 17,032, q = 27,995, N = 1013): C3a(V) > 1.19229904840497896133 (+0.00127095 over 1.19102809).

3.4. Combinatorial Designs, Fast Matrix Multiplication, DNA Minimizers, Graphs & Lattice Gases

  • Covering Designs (20 records, PRs #397, #398, #435): Generalized staircase composition + C bitset prune.c and pair-to-single merge.c lowered C(94,13,5) from 129,203 to 125,184 (−4,019 blocks), C(68,14,5) to 15,675 (−329), C(40,16,7) to 7,251 (−219), and 17 additional cells, each verified 1-minimal (0 redundant blocks).
  • Ternary Fast Matrix Multiplication (4 records in ℤT = {−1, 0, 1}, PRs #402, #432): Signed permutation group B4 sub-block conjugation made all 20 unshared terms of ⟨4,3,3⟩ = ⟨2,3,3⟩⊕⟨2,3,3⟩ 100% sign-pure, yielding ⟨9,12,14⟩ rank 954 (vs. 960), ⟨14,15,15⟩ rank 1,894 (beating both ℤT=1,905 and ℤ=1,895), ⟨12,15,15⟩ rank 1,640 (vs. 1,650), and ⟨10,15,15⟩ rank 1,389 (vs. 1,395).
  • DNA Minimizer Density (17 records, PRs #425, #433): Exact Sum-Over-Subsets Fast Zeta Transform (SOS-DP) over tie-seam gamechanger DAGs lowered (w=12, k=4) to 573,933,439 / 232 = 0.1336292920 (−1.7763% rel.), (w=12, k=3) to 0.1365689458 (−1.3155% rel.), and 15 additional cells by up to −4.274%.
  • Degree–Diameter Graphs & Lattice Gases (PRs #366, #457, #462): Constructed a (Δ=14, D=3) graph on N = 1,118 vertices (7,809 edges) via ℤ16 voltage lift + 22-vertex seam patch surgery, and computed A054759(15) = 4310182149988584261406143524214457416387448 plus all 80 terms of antidiagonal d=79 of A089980 (peaking at the 279-digit T(39,40)) via two independent modular transfer decompositions across 75 disjoint primes.
